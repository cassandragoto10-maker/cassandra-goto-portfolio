01 — OVERVIEW

Understanding how particles fluidize.

Fluidization occurs when a fluid is passed upward through a bed of particles at a sufficient velocity for the particles to become suspended by the fluid.

The experiment investigated the relationship between water flow rate, superficial velocity and pressure drop across a packed bed of silica sand.

Experimental observations and calculated values were used to identify the minimum fluidization velocity and assess the resulting fluidization behaviour.

03 — EXPERIMENT

Experimental system.

The experimental system consisted of a vertical fluidization column containing silica sand. Water was introduced from the bottom of the column and passed upward through the particle bed.

Pressure measurements were taken across the bed at different water flow rates. The measured values were subsequently converted and analysed using Python.

05 — RESULTS

Pressure drop behaviour.

The pressure-drop profile provides a direct indication of the transition from a packed bed towards fluidized behaviour as the water flow rate increases.

Figure 1: Comparison of experimental pressure drop with theoretical pressure-drop relationships.

Experimental minimum fluidization

The experimental minimum fluidization velocity was identified as approximately 0.01886 m/s, corresponding to a water flow rate of 1.8 L/min.

At this point, the measured pressure drop was approximately 490.33 Pa. The calculated Froude number was approximately 0.04534.

06 — MODEL COMPARISON

Comparing experimental and theoretical behaviour.

EXPERIMENTAL Measured data

Pressure drop was obtained directly from differential pressure measurements across the fluidized bed.

This provides the experimental basis against which theoretical relationships can be compared.

ERGUN Packed-bed model

The Ergun equation was used to estimate pressure drop based on viscous and inertial contributions through the particle bed.

The model provides a theoretical reference for pressure losses before and around fluidization.

CARMAN–KOZENY Laminar model

The Carman–Kozeny relationship was used as another theoretical comparison for pressure drop through the packed particle bed.

Comparing the models helps evaluate how closely theoretical predictions represent the experiment.

07 — INTERPRETATION
OBSERVED BEHAVIOUR Particulate

Fluidization regime

The experimental system exhibited particulate fluidization behaviour, characterised by relatively smooth and uniform particle motion.

The calculated Froude number and experimental observations were used together to interpret the fluidization behaviour rather than relying on a single calculated quantity.

08 — LIMITATIONS & IMPROVEMENTS

What could be improved?

  • Repeat measurements at each flow rate to quantify experimental variability.
  • Use a more automated method for determining the onset of fluidization.
  • Investigate uncertainty associated with pressure measurements and flow-rate readings.
  • Compare additional theoretical correlations where appropriate.
  • Improve visualisation of particle behaviour using recorded experimental observations.
